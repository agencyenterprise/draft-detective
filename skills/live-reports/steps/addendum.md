# Step 5a: Addendum

Write an addendum to publish with the report: a section for the report's own readers that tells them what new information bears on the report and how it changes what the report says. It sits next to the original, on the report's page or at its end, and leaves the original text untouched.

The change plan (step 5b) speaks to the authors and says what to edit. The addendum speaks to the readers and says what has changed. The user may ask for either one or both.

You are given the report, the intake, the approved research brief, and the user's answers to the brief's questions for the authors, if any.

## Who you are writing for

The report's readers: the same audience, with the same background. They know the report's subject, but may not remember the report well, and they don't know this update's process.

- Write in the report's register, with its terms and abbreviations, spelled out at first use as the report does.
- Don't use the update's working labels: no item ids (`C3`, `F1`), no verdict words ("superseded", "authors' call"), no mention of research plans, notes or briefs.
- Assume a reader who reads only the opening paragraph and the headings. Both must carry the main changes on their own.

## What goes in

- **A heading, a date line and a note on how it was made.** "Addendum: what has changed since this report", then the date of the addendum and how far its sources reach ("Covers public sources published through <month year>"), then, on its own line, "Prepared with AI-assisted research."
- **An opening paragraph,** at most 100 words: what the report examined and when, that this addendum summarizes the developments since that bear most on its findings and recommendations, and what it does not do. Say plainly what wasn't redone: the authors' own data, models, interviews or analysis. It describes the world since the report, not a new study.
- **One section per change that matters,** ordered by how much it affects the report's conclusions, not by the report's structure. Head each with a plain statement of the change ("The JAIC has been replaced"). In one paragraph of about 60 to 120 words: what the report said, in a clause; what is true now, with dates; and what it means for the report's findings or recommendations. Merge related changes into one section rather than listing every item.
- **Where evidence disagrees,** say so in the section it belongs to: who reports what, and that the question is open. Don't resolve it.
- **What still holds.** One short paragraph naming the findings that remain accurate, each with its source. Readers need to know what they can still rely on, not only what changed.
- **Sources.** A numbered list, `[1]`, `[2]`…, of the sources the addendum cites, in the order they first appear, with full citations and links taken from the brief. Cite only sources the brief cites.

Aim for 500 to 900 words above the sources, by how much has changed. A report with little change gets a short addendum.

## Rules

- **Use only what the brief contains,** and the user's answers. Don't add facts, sources or dates from memory, and don't reopen the research notes for new material.
- **Follow the user's answers.** Where the user answered a question for the authors, write the addendum accordingly. Where a claim was left to the authors and they haven't answered, describe the evidence without concluding what it means for that claim.
- **Don't speak for the authors.** The addendum reports developments; it doesn't withdraw or endorse the report's conclusions. Where a change undermines a conclusion, say what changed and that the conclusion rests on it, and leave the verdict to the authors.
- **Say when a figure isn't comparable.** When a newer figure is measured differently from the report's (another scale, benchmark version or method), say so in a clause.
- If the `voice-and-tone` skill is available, follow its author voice: this is text the authors will publish as their own.

# Addendum: what has changed since this report

*Prepared with AI-assisted research.*

<What the report said; what is true now, with dates; what it means for its findings or recommendations.> [1] [2]
