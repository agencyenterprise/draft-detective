# Step 5b: Change plan

Turn the approved brief into a plan for revising the report: what to keep, what to change, what to add and what to remove, section by section, and on what evidence. Describe each change; don't draft the new text yet.

## How to plan

- **Go section by section,** in the report's order, covering every section in scope. A section with nothing to change still appears, marked keep, so the user sees it was considered.
- **One item per change, kept short.** Each one says in one or two sentences what to do, where, and why, and cites the claim or fact it comes from (`C2`, `F3`) and at most two of the brief's sources (`[S2]`). The brief has the evidence; the plan says what to do with it.
- **Group what stays.** In a section, put passages that stay as they are, or only need a dated mention of what happened since, together in one "Keep" item.
- **What wasn't researched.** Facts and data the research plan left out ("Not included") have no evidence behind them. Don't plan changes to them; list them once, under "Across the report", as passages the authors should check themselves or mark as dated.
- **Actions:**
  - **Keep:** no change needed.
  - **Revise:** the passage stays, with updated facts, figures or framing.
  - **Add:** new material, such as a paragraph or a subsection, for a development the report doesn't cover.
  - **Remove:** material that no longer holds and that a revision can't save.
  - **Restructure:** move, merge or split passages because the argument's shape changed.
- **Follow the consequences up the layers.** Newer data changes a figure; a changed fact changes the passages that state it; a weakened claim changes the passages that argue it, and the summary and recommendations that rely on it. Start from the brief's judgment of each claim, trace it through the report, and plan the knock-on changes too. A claim that stands with updates needs only its facts refreshed; one that no longer holds needs its argument revised or removed.
- **Report-level changes.** Consider the title, the summary or abstract, the recommendations and the conclusions, and a note on the revision date.
- **The user's answers.** Where the user answered the brief's questions for the authors, follow their answers; where they left one open, plan it as a "Check" item.
- **Disagreements.** For a contested fact or a contrary view in the brief, plan to acknowledge the disagreement in the report, not to pick a side, unless the user has.
- **Respect the authors' choices.** Keep the report's scope, method and argument unless the evidence forces a change. When a change would alter a conclusion, say so plainly: that is the authors' call.

## Write `change-plan.md`

```markdown
# Change plan

<Two or three sentences: how much changes, and which changes matter most.>

## <Section heading>

- **Revise.** <What to change and how.> Because <reason>. (C2, F3) [S2]
- **Keep.** <What stays, briefly, with any dated mention to add.>

## <Next section heading>

…

## Across the report

- **Revise.** <Summary, recommendations, title, revision note…>
- **Check.** <Passages resting on facts the research plan left out, and claims the brief left to the authors, for them to check or mark as dated.>
```

Aim for a plan the user can review in five minutes: usually one to four items per section.

Then tell the user which changes matter most, especially any that alter a conclusion, and ask them to approve the plan or change it. When they approve it, the plan is complete: drafting the revised text is a separate task.
