Four-Day Workweek Pilots in Municipal Government

Jordan Ellis, Priya Raman

9

Summary

11

Between 2023 and 2024, six mid-sized cities moved part of their municipal workforce to a 32-hour, four-day schedule with no change in pay. This report reviews what the pilots measured and what the results can tell other cities.

15

Findings

17

Sick-leave use dropped by 18 percent across the pilot cities, which shows that the four-day week caused a lasting improvement in staff health.

19

It was decided by the steering committee that call-centre staff would be excluded from the pilot, because minimum phone coverage could not otherwise be guaranteed.

21

Any city that still refuses to adopt a four-day week is simply ignoring overwhelming evidence.

23

Service-request backlogs fell in five of the six cities during the pilot year. Compressed schedules consistently improve service delivery in every department that adopts them (Okafor and Brandt, 2022).

25

Resident satisfaction surveys were run in four of the six cities. Scores were flat in three and rose slightly in one, and none of the surveys had a comparison group.

27

Recommendations

29
  • City managers should run any new pilot for at least two budget cycles and publish the service metrics they track.
  • Departments with fixed coverage requirements should pilot staggered schedules before a four-day week.
