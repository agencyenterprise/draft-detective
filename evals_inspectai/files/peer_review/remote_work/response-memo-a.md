# Response to Reviewer A: "Distributed Work and Team Productivity"

Thank you for the detailed review. Your comments are repeated below in order, each followed by our response.

## Framing

**The title promises evidence about productivity, but the report measures a constructed index.**

Response: Agreed. We retitled the report around coordination and added a note to the About This section saying that the "delivery index" is one specific construct and not a measure of productivity in any broader sense.

**Two sentences explaining selection bias in plain terms.**

Response: The Background section now explains, in plain terms, why teams that choose remote work may differ from teams that do not, and how a panel design lets each team act as its own comparison.

## Methods

**The Methods section is far longer than this audience needs.**

Response: We have kept the section in the body. The other reviewer asked for more detail on the sampling frame and on what drove the arrangement changes, and we think a reader needs that detail in front of them to judge whether the design identifies anything. We say this in the Methods section itself.

**Voluntary recruitment through an industry consortium is a real limitation.**

Response: The Discussion now carries an explicit limitation paragraph on consortium recruitment and on the sectors studied.

## Interpretation

**The "coordination cost rather than an effort cost" claim.**

Response: We softened this to a hypothesis and now name reviewer load as an alternative explanation we have not tested.

**The team-age finding turned directly into Recommendation 2.**

Response: We have dropped Recommendation 2. The team-age result is now reported only as a hypothesis for future study.

**Recommendation 3 is an inference beyond the data.**

Response: We have kept Recommendation 3 as written. Two of the twelve panel firms ran latency-reduction programs in 2023 and shared their before-and-after cycle-time data with us under the same agreement as the panel; both recovered most of the cycle-time difference within two quarters. Those data are not part of this study's sample, which is why they are not reported in the text, but they are why we are comfortable making the recommendation.

## Minor comments

**"Available on request" is not an acceptable reference list.**

Response: We added a full reference list.
