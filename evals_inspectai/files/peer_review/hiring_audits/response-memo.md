Response to reviewer comments on "Auditing Algorithmic Hiring Tools"

We thank the reviewer for a careful reading. Each comment is repeated below, followed by our response.

1. (p. 1) About This vs. Capacity tension.

   Response: We rewrote the About This section. It now says the report assumes no technical background but concludes that agencies need technical staff they do not yet have, and that the final section is an argument for hiring them.

2. (p. 2) Unsupported claim that vendors "converged on a narrow reading".

   Response: We now report that we reviewed all 41 published Local Law 144 audit summaries as of March 2024, with counts for each pattern, and state that this is the population rather than a sample.

3. (p. 3) Layer 2 is underdeveloped.

   Response: We added a plain-language definition of construct validity. We also added an evidence threshold: auditors should require a criterion validity coefficient of at least 0.3 from a study on a comparable population. Finally, we added a paragraph reconciling Layer 2 with the Uniform Guidelines on Employee Selection Procedures, explaining where the framework goes beyond them.

4. (p. 3) Prosody example doing double duty.

   Response: We kept the example as an illustration. We searched the EEOC and state enforcement dockets and found no published audit finding on prosody-based scoring, so there is nothing in the public record we could add without inventing one.

5. (p. 4) Legal basis for treating non-production as a finding.

   Response: We added a paragraph on authority, grounding the finding in existing recordkeeping rules and distinguishing a record finding from a discrimination finding. Recommendation 3 now says the same.

7. (p. 5) Cost of Recommendation 4.

   Response: We added a cost estimate to the Capacity section: 12 regional offices in scope, two staff each, for a total of roughly $3.1 million a year.

8. (p. 5) No reference list.

   Response: We added a reference list.
