# Repurposing Retiring Power Plants for Data Centers

## Summary

This report assesses whether sites of retiring power plants could host data centers for artificial intelligence (AI) training. We assessed 14 candidate sites against five criteria and ranked them in three tiers. In order to rank the sites, we combined public grid data with interviews of 22 planners.

Retiring plants keep their grid connection, and that connection is the scarcest input a data center needs. The sites in the top tier could each host at least 300 megawatts of load within three years. This is a complex issue.

## How We Built the Framework

In this section, we describe the framework and how we applied it. The initial step in developing the framework involved identifying the categories that matter most for power availability. We first chose five categories: grid connection, generation on site, water, transmission headroom, and permitting. Planners we interviewed said that permitting timelines vary more between states than any other factor.

It is important to note that the five categories are weighted equally. We tested alternative weights and the tiers did not change, so we kept the equal weighting for the sake of transparency.

Table 1 lists the sites. Sites in the top tier are located in the Midwest, and most of them retired coal units between 2019 and 2024. The three sites in the second tier are situated in different states, the retiring assets at these locations rely on coal for power generation, the generating units range from 39 to 55 years old.

There were few instances when a site managed to achieve an A in the weighted scoring, with most sites receiving a C. This reflects the age of the transmission equipment at most sites. The scores may understate the value of sites where a utility has announced upgrades, because we scored the equipment as it stands.

## Community Considerations

The framework prioritizes locations where development can meaningfully address community needs, foster economic growth, and minimize negative impacts. We scored community fit on two indicators: the share of local jobs lost when the plant closed, and whether the county has an active redevelopment plan. Nine of the 14 counties have such a plan.

Every idea above pulls in a direction. Stakeholders have different perspectives on data centers, and the reader should keep that in mind. Planners in three counties said that residents worry about water use more than about noise or traffic.

## Conclusion

There are multiple sites across the United States where existing infrastructure can be made available for AI data centers by repurposing plants slated for retirement. No single site has characteristics that make it uniquely suited to development; every site comes with tradeoffs. We recommend that developers start with the top-tier sites and that state agencies publish their permitting timelines, since the interviews suggest timelines are the main source of uncertainty.
