# Mobility Vouchers in the Lakeshore Region

## Introduction

The Lakeshore Housing Authority offers mobility vouchers that help families with children move to neighborhoods with lower poverty rates. Between 2021 and 2024, 1,150 families received one. The authority asked us to evaluate the program's first three years. This report examines who used the vouchers, the neighborhoods families chose, and how their children fared in school. In this report, we first describe the program and our data, then present our analysis, and close with implications for the authority.

## Program and Data

The authority's vouchers cover the difference between a family's rent contribution and the market rent in eligible neighborhoods. Each family also receives up to 12 months of counseling on searching for housing. We used the authority's voucher records, school enrollment data from four districts, and interviews with 30 families and 11 counselors.

## Overview

Of the 1,150 families who received a mobility voucher, 790 used it to move within the voucher's 120-day search period. Families whose counselor had worked at the authority for more than two years were more likely to move than families assigned to newer counselors. The families who did not move most often cited a lack of landlords willing to accept the voucher.

## Where Families Moved

Most families who moved went to neighborhoods with poverty rates below 10 percent, compared with 34 percent in the neighborhoods they left. Two-thirds moved within the same school district, and a third moved to a different one.

**Distance.** Families moved a median of 6 miles from their previous address, and few moved more than 15 miles.

## School Outcomes

Children who moved to a different school district had higher attendance in their first year, but their test scores did not change. Children who stayed in the same district showed no change in either attendance or scores. With two years of data for most children, it is too early to say whether the attendance gain will last or whether scores will follow.

## Landlord Participation

**Counselors spent much of their time recruiting landlords.** In interviews, 8 of the 11 counselors said they spent more time persuading landlords to accept vouchers than helping families search.

## Other Issues

Counselors had caseloads of up to 60 families, twice the authority's target of 30. Families assigned to counselors with caseloads above 45 took a median of 20 days longer to find housing.

## Implications for the Authority

Most families who received vouchers moved to lower-poverty neighborhoods, and counselors appear to be central to whether a family moves at all. The authority could bring caseloads toward its target of 30 families per counselor and expand its outreach to landlords. The school outcomes are too early to judge.
