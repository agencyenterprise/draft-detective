# Reducing Water Losses at Midvale Water Utility

## Key Findings

- Midvale lost 27 percent of the water it treated in 2023, well above the 15 percent the state considers acceptable.
- Acoustic leak detection found 340 leaks in its first year and cut losses to 21 percent.
- The 2024 rate increase raised the average household bill by $6 a month.
- Fewer than one in five eligible households enrolled in the customer assistance program.
- Billing errors, not staffing shortages, caused most of the delays in repairing leaks that customers reported.

## Introduction

Midvale Water Utility serves 180,000 customers in three counties. In 2023, its board asked us to examine why the utility loses so much of the water it treats and what the losses cost customers. This report describes the utility's losses, the programs it launched in 2023 and 2024 to reduce them, and their effects on customer bills. Chapter 2 describes our approach, Chapter 3 presents what we found, and Chapter 4 discusses what it means for the board.

## Chapter 2: Our Approach

We analyzed the utility's production and billing records for 2019 to 2024, reviewed work orders for 5,200 leak repairs, and interviewed 16 utility staff. We also surveyed 600 households in the utility's service area in spring 2024.

**Production and billing records.** Production records measure the water leaving the treatment plants; billing records measure the water customers pay for. The difference is the utility's water loss.

**Staff interviews.** We interviewed operations staff, billing staff and the utility's three district managers about how leaks are reported, routed and repaired.

## Chapter 3: Findings

### Losses Were Well Above the State Standard

Midvale lost 27 percent of the water it treated in 2023. The state considers losses above 15 percent a sign that a system needs repair. Losses were highest in the oldest district, where most mains were laid before 1960.

### Leak Detection

The utility began acoustic leak detection in January 2024. Crews found 340 leaks in the first year, most of them in mains that showed no sign of leaking at the surface. Losses fell to 21 percent by December 2024, the lowest level since 2015.

**Crews.** Repair crews fixed the leaks found by acoustic detection in an average of 9 days, compared with 31 days for leaks reported by customers.

### Rate Changes

The board raised rates 8 percent in July 2024. For the average household, the increase added $6 to the monthly bill. Households in the lowest income quintile spent 3.1 percent of their income on water after the increase, up from 2.8 percent.

### Customer Assistance Programs Reached Few Eligible Households

About 14,000 households qualified for the utility's assistance program in 2024, but only 2,500 enrolled. **Most eligible households did not know the program existed.** In our survey, 68 percent of eligible respondents had not heard of it.

### Staffing

Most of the delay in repairing customer-reported leaks came from the billing system, not from crew availability. Leak reports entered through the billing system took an average of 12 days to reach a repair crew, because the system routed them as billing disputes. Reports made by phone to the operations desk reached a crew within a day.

## Chapter 4: Discussion

Acoustic detection has already cut losses by a fifth, and the board could extend it to the two districts it does not yet cover. Routing leak reports directly to operations, rather than through billing, would remove most of the repair delay for customer reports. The assistance program reaches few of the households the rate increase affects most, which suggests that outreach, not the program's design, is what limits it.

## Appendix A: Survey Instrument

The household survey asked respondents about their water bills, their awareness of the assistance program and their experience reporting leaks.
