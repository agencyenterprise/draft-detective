# Evaluation of the Lakeside Home Visit Program

## Chapter 1. Introduction

This chapter describes the program and the questions our evaluation addressed. The Lakeside Home Visit Program (the program) sends community health workers to the homes of patients with diabetes who have recently left the hospital. Before the program launched, the county has relied on hospital social workers for home follow-up, and most patients received one call after discharge. The pilot phase ran from January 2021 to December 2024, and the county now funds the program from its general budget.

Medicaid covers home visits in the state only when a physician orders them. In 2023, the program has added a second clinic in the northern part of the county. Since 2022, the program has employed 14 community health workers, and the program continues to operate in both parts of the county.

## Chapter 2. Methods

We reviewed enrollment records for 640 patients and interviewed 22 community health workers and 35 patients between March and July 2025. At baseline, most enrolled patients have missed at least one clinic appointment in the previous six months. We compared refill records for enrolled patients with refill records for 410 patients at a county without a home visit program.

## Chapter 3. Results

Since the program began in 2021, community health workers completed more than 3,000 home visits, and the visits continue at about 90 a month. Enrolled patients filled 81 percent of their prescriptions on time, compared with 64 percent in the comparison county. Patients reported that the visits helped them understand their medications.

During the pilot phase, the program has strengthened referrals between the hospital and the two clinics, and clinic staff told us that discharge summaries now arrive within a day. Several community health workers said the caseloads are still too large for weekly visits. Patients may not notice these changes directly, but they appear in the refill data.

## Chapter 4. Recommendations

Research on community health worker programs suggests that home visits improve medication adherence; in particular, patients with diabetes reported fewer missed doses when visits were monthly (Hale and Ortiz, 2020). Hale and Ortiz (2020) found that monthly visits worked best for older patients. Future programs would benefit from hiring community health workers who live in the neighborhoods they serve.

The county should keep the monthly schedule for patients over 65 and test a shorter schedule for younger patients.
